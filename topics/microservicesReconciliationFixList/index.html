    F. Auth / security posture (decide, then small change)¶

    • Six unauthenticated gRPC doors (Validator, Block Assembly, Block Validation, Subtree Validation, Pruner, Propagation) with security_level_grpc = 0: either wire auth or state "the private network is the perimeter" in settings.conf (§78, Q55) — PARKED (maintainer posture): the facts are verified (only Blockchain, Legacy and P2P install the grpc_admin_api_key interceptor; the other six start with nothing to check and nothing encrypts them). Which way to state it is the decision: wiring the key into six services is a deployment-coupling change (every interior caller needs the key), while the prose option blesses the current topology. Needs one answer: is any gRPC port of a production deployment reachable by anything that is not a trusted service?
    • Empty rpc_user/rpc_pass: choose fail-closed vs auth-disabled; fix the losing sentence (see A3, E3) (§60, Q37) — PARKED (maintainer posture): today's behaviour is fail-closed and E3's tests (auth_empty_credentials_test.go) now pin it, so the risk of drifting accidentally is gone; what remains is a deliberate choice. Keeping fail-closed means rewriting the "Leave empty to disable authentication" longdesc of rpc_user/rpc_pass to say empty locks the door. Choosing auth-disabled changes what every development context without credentials means (anything listening could be driven) and would rewrite E3's assertions instead of the prose. Needs one answer: should a node with no credentials accept unauthenticated RPC at all?
    • maxrawtxfee: add to settings.conf, or move the policy into the Validator so it applies to every door, not just RPC (§57, Q36) — added to settings.conf beside the RPC credentials with its units, bypass, default provenance (bitcoin-sv DEFAULT_TRANSACTION_MAXFEE) and the honest note that the validator and P2P paths accept the same transaction unchecked; moving the rule into the Validator remains the open half, recorded in the comment itself
    • Heavy-route anonymous ceiling: decide whether the two-bucket split doubling 10 → 20 sustained is intended; if not, halve the default (§47, Q27) — PARKED (maintainer intent): the split's purpose (a catching-up peer keeps its floor while a bulk caller floods another heavy route) is documented and honest about the cost; the start-up line even prints the doubled number. What is not recorded anywhere is whether 20/s sustained per unverified IP across the heavy surface is an accepted capacity figure or an oversight of the split. Halving asset_httpHeavyRateLimit would restore the old total but also halve the floor catch-up was the point of protecting. Needs one answer: is the doubled anonymous ceiling accepted, or should the default move to 5?
    • Limited-tier allowlist: prune the 19 of 44 unimplemented methods, or label the list as granting permission not capability (§59, Q39)
